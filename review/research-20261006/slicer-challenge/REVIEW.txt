Adversarial mechanical assessment:
The native design's nominal residual inward movement allowance is about
0.0273 mm. Exact CAD contact is tiny and concentrated on nub flanks near the
receiving lip, not a broad, centered bearing patch. Existing fine-mesh probes
show the contacted sloping roof faces have essentially zero normal component
along the intended inward bending direction. Rounded lip transitions, friction,
and elastic deformation could alter the actual load path; the current CAD contact
alone does not prove that insertion drives the intended inward bend.

The nominally flexing region remains joined to full hex walls and approximately
1.45 mm of back skin. It must be assessed as a coupled wall/plate structure, not
as a free cantilever. There is no validated force or strain calculation here.
The tiny preload/contact geometry is sensitive to mesh, wall generation, layer
registration and physical dimensional error. Do not assume CAD interference
survives as a repeatable printed preload, or that no warnings implies retention.

Recommendations BEFORE any geometry change:
Keep current geometry for the first controlled fit test. Use fine export,
Classic walls, 0.16 mm layers, four walls, and the saved 0.4 mm PETG setup.
Use 0.12 mm as a comparison if receiver stair steps cause rough engagement;
the clip-only finer-layer test did not materially improve maximum nub extension.
Measure insertion/removal force, observe the actual inward deflection, check
fully seated rattle/play, inspect damage, then repeat after cycling and a seated
dwell. Test PLA separately; a successful PLA slice is not mechanical validation.
Use multiple specimens and record printer/filament/process settings so a single
good fit does not mask sensitivity. Agree on application load requirements before
declaring retention force acceptable.

If fit is stiff or damage appears, first revise local flex thickness and the
hidden receiving lip/load direction while preserving full walls and no grooves.
Do not simply enlarge the nub or increase interference. If it seats loosely,
first measure the printed contact geometry before choosing a preload adjustment.
The housing's external hex interface should stay uniform.
