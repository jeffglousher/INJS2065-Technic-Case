# Coupon review — 2026-11-16

An independent general reviewer approved the six-piece plate for **fit screening**.
It did not accept printed retention, strength or service life. No supported defect
justified changing the mating geometry before testing the complete coupon set.

The combined project preserves the validated source meshes, all original print
orientations and object layer bands. Fresh Bambu Studio 2.9.1 slices report six
objects, one PETG filament, zero color changes and no clearance warnings. Maximum
mesh height is 24.45 mm, below the saved 32.8 mm rod-clearance height. The native
sequential clearance check also passes. All three receivers retain 2 mm brims;
clips remain solid and brim-free. Automated checks confirm open backing slots,
continuous tongues and exact source layer schedules.

The reviewer independently checked all twelve original nub stations in the
combined G-code, including straight segments and arcs. At print Z=2.28 mm,
0.42 mm beads reach 0.4747–0.4797 mm from each nub axis, compared with the modeled
0.48 mm crest radius. All four nubs survive on every clip. Both new hooks have
extrusion at Z=3.56, 3.72, 3.88 and 4.04 mm. This does not prove the physical
features printed correctly in the earlier run.

Seated lower/upper coupon mesh intersections have zero positive volume for the
control and both latch variants; the original seam remains at native Z=18.25 mm.
The A/B cuts remain away from the four original end nubs. The local slots and
opened pocket walls intentionally free the spring tongue.

Release remains a physical-test question. The existing proof rigidly translates
an isolated hook by 0.5 mm. A simple cantilever-shaped displacement sensitivity
check leaves about 0.00023 mm³ withdrawal overlap at 0.5 mm tip motion and clears
at 0.55 mm, against a nominal 0.6 mm flex gap. Neither calculation is a structural
simulation. Do not promise a precise tool stroke or force a printed tongue
against its stop. Reject a coupon that cannot release cleanly without damage.

The plate map and instructions now identify every part and its matching test
pair. IDs are not embossed: mark the pieces before removal. Printed acceptance
still requires full seating, a distinct catch, no perceptible play, blocked
withdrawal without release, clean tool release and the initial ten-cycle screen.
Full shells and representative motor loads remain necessary after that screen.
